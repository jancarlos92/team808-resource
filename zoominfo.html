ZoomInfo Saved Search Playbook

Step-by-step setup for Advanced Search: the exact filters, the exact values, and the copy-paste blocks for all 12 ICP lanes. These searches target the accounts that look like the ones we've already won.

Search Philosophy NAICS identifies the business type. ICP layers identify the accounts worth a BDR's time. Prioritize operating complexity — multiple locations, entities, plants, branches, terminals, facilities, projects, or states — then add a timely growth or finance trigger. Technology is a prioritization layer, never a hard gate.

The System — 4 Saved Searches Per Lane + 1 Contact Search

Build A once, then clone it three times. Name them exactly like this so the whole team's searches match: "GB-01 Trucking — A Base", "— B +Legacy Finance", "— C Growth Scoops", "— D Finance & Reorg Scoops", "— Contacts Finance".

A · BASE ICP

NAICS + size + territory + keywords. Your account universe.

B · +LEGACY FINANCE

Clone A, add legacy finance tech. Maturity-gap accounts.

C · GROWTH SCOOPS

Clone A, add expansion/opening/new-market Scoops. The business is outgrowing its stack.

D · FINANCE & REORG SCOOPS

Clone A, add M&A, PE, new-CFO and system-change Scoops. Act-now triggers.

Step-by-Step — Build Search A (Base ICP)

Open ZoomInfo → Advanced Search. The filter names below match the left-hand panel exactly. Pick your lane in the next section first so you have the blocks ready to paste.

1

Open the Companies panel Companies

Advanced Search builds from the left filter rail. You'll work top to bottom through the Companies group first — account filters before people filters, always.

2

Set the industry by NAICS Companies → Industry

Click Industry, switch to the NAICS Codes input, and paste your lane's NAICS block (copy button below). Paste the whole comma-separated block at once — ZoomInfo parses it.

NAICS defines the universe. Everything after this step narrows it to accounts worth your time.

3

Set company size Companies → Employee Count

Enter your lane's employee range as a starting point (most lanes: 50–1,500; restaurants 100–5,000). Treat it soft — widen it when the lane guidance says complexity beats headcount.

4

Territory + HQ only Companies → Location + Corporate Hierarchy

Set your territory in the location filter. Then open Corporate Hierarchy and select headquarters / parent-level records only.

This kills duplicate branch records — you want the parent operating company where finance decisions live, not 40 individual locations of the same brand.

5

Tighten with keywords Companies → Company Keywords

Paste your lane's keyword string (with the OR operators and quotes intact). Critical for broad NAICS families — especially hospitality, where the codes alone are too wide.

6

Optional revenue soft gate Companies → Company Revenue

~$10M–$500M for General Business, ~$20M–$500M for Hospitality. Soft means: never remove an account solely for being outside the band when complexity is strong.

7

Save it as Search A Save Search

Save with the team naming convention: "[Lane] — A Base". Set the alert to weekly so new matching accounts land in your inbox.

Clone Into B, C, and D

B

Search B — Legacy Finance Stack Sales Signals → Technologies

Open Search A → Save As "— B +Legacy Finance". In Sales Signals → Technologies, add your lane's legacy-finance list (QuickBooks, Dynamics GP, etc.). Weekly alert.

These are the accounts most like the ones we've already won: a growing, complex business still running an SMB finance stack. A missing technographic record never disqualifies — it just doesn't boost.

C

Search C — Growth Scoops Sales Signals → Scoops

Clone A → "— C Growth Scoops". Open Scoops, set the date range to a rolling ~6 months (like the pinned example), and select your lane's growth topics: expansions, openings, new locations/markets, development agreements.

Growth scoops mean the business is getting more complex — new entities, locations, and consolidation pain arriving on a schedule. These accounts are outgrowing their stack in real time. Weekly alert works; the window stays relevant for months.

D

Search D — Finance & Reorg Scoops Sales Signals → Scoops

Clone A → "— D Finance & Reorg Scoops". Same Scoops filter, same date range — but select the finance and reorganization topics: acquisition, merger, private equity, new CFO / Controller, finance hiring, ERP or system change.

These are the act-now triggers: a new finance leader reevaluates everything in their first 90 days, and M&A forces a systems conversation. Set this alert to daily — these age fastest. New CFO at an account that's also in Search B or C is the best call on your list.

Scoops — How to Read the Triggers

BucketScoopsWhat You Do
C · GrowthExpansion · New location/facility/terminal/plant/property · Openings · New market · Development agreements · Renovation · New concept/product lineThe business is compounding complexity. Lead with the growth story: "congrats on X — how is finance keeping up?"
D · Finance & ReorgAcquisition · Merger · Private equity · Divestiture · New CFO / Controller · Finance hiring · ERP / system implementation · Shared services / centralizationAct now. Find the finance leader immediately — new leaders reevaluate everything in their first 90 days.
Best comboAccount appears in B or C and DLegacy stack or active growth plus a finance trigger = top of the call list, today.
🚫 Never Say "I Saw You Use X" (or "I Saw Your Scoop") Technographic and Scoop data is for prioritizing and forming a hypothesis — not for the opener. Lead with a business question about scale, consolidation, reporting, or integration complexity instead. The data told you where to look; the pain prop is still how you open. Congratulating public news (a new location, an acquisition) is fine — citing their software stack is not.
